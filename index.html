A Clear Agenda for Tyrone

The Strategic Vision

Three carefully considered pillars — each one reflecting what the people of Tyrone value most: their parks, their culture, and the quiet efficiency of a city that simply works.

Pillar One

Quality of Life

Enhancing the scenic parks and green spaces that define Tyrone's character — from Shamrock Park to Fabon Brown Park. Mayor Dial believes that exceptional public spaces are not an amenity but a right, and is committed to ensuring Tyrone's outdoor environment remains among the finest in Fayette County.

Pillar Two

Cultural Excellence

Supporting the local institutions that give Tyrone its soul — The Legacy Theatre, the Tyrone Museum, and the arts and cultural programming that make this community more than just a place to live. Mayor Dial is committed to sustaining and growing the cultural infrastructure that sets Tyrone apart.

Pillar Three

Smart Infrastructure

Modernizing city services with the technology and efficiency residents expect — without sacrificing the small-town charm that makes Tyrone irreplaceable. Digital city access, improved roads, streamlined permitting, and responsive government: progress that feels invisible because it simply works.

About Mayor Dial

Rooted in Tyrone.
Dedicated to its Excellence.

Mayor Eric Dial is a committed public servant and proud resident of Tyrone, Georgia. His leadership is defined by the same qualities that define Tyrone itself: precision, care, and an uncompromising standard for the quality of life every resident deserves.

With a background in [insert field — e.g., public administration, business, civic leadership], Mayor Dial has devoted himself to the proposition that good government is not loud — it is effective. Under his stewardship, Tyrone's parks, cultural institutions, and city services have become benchmarks for communities across Fayette County.

From championing The Legacy Theatre and the Tyrone Museum to investing in Shamrock Park and Fabon Brown Park, Mayor Dial understands that a city's character is measured not in square miles but in the quality of its spaces, its institutions, and its governance.

He believes that Tyrone's most valuable resource is not any single park or building — it is the community itself. And he is committed to making sure city hall never takes that for granted.
